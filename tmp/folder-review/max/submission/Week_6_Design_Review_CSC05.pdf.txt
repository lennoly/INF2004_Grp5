1. Requirements
State what the system must do and the important quality or timing constraints. Requirements should be specific, 
testable, and traceable to the proposed design (you may add more FR and/or NFR as needed).
1.1 Functional Requirements
ID Requirement
FR1 Follow the 18 mm black line with two analogue IR sensors and PID steering (state LINE_FOLLOW).
FR2 Confirm line loss after 150 ms with both sensors on white; search with up to five alternating sweeps of growing angle 
(±k·30°, k = 1…5), starting towards the last-seen side; stop with "line not found" if unsuccessful.
FR3
Read Code 39 barcodes (*A*–*D*) beside the line in either reading direction: A = left, B = right, C = straight at the next 
junction; D = U-turn at once. Discard and report a junction command not used within 400 mm. Report other valid 
symbols (e.g. *Z*) without manoeuvring.
FR4 Execute encoder-controlled moves: forward / backward a distance, turn left / right an angle, stop.
FR5 Detect and count humps from IMU pitch; report each peak height and the run maximum.
FR6 Report pitch, roll, heading, turn rate and motion class (stationary, cruising, accelerating, decelerating, turning, climbing, 
descending, impact); detect impacts from the accelerometer.
FR7
While driving forward, treat an obstacle as ahead after two consecutive valid front readings < 300 mm or an IMU 
impact; stop, scan (coarse 30–150°, fine ±20° in 5° steps) and estimate the obstacle's edges and side clearances. 
Each ping is VALID (a range), OUT_OF_RANGE (echo pulse longer than 4 m; treated as free space only if A9 confirms 
the module gives this pulse when nothing is in range) or FAULT (no echo pulse). Two consecutive FAULTs, or a front 
reading older than 200 ms while driving forward, stop the car with "sensor fault".
FR8
Bypass on the side needing the smaller offset, or reverse and re-plan (2 attempts shared), or stop with "path blocked"; 
then rejoin the line. Every forward leg (SIDESTEP, PASS, RETURN, REJOIN) starts only after a 70° / 90° / 110° check 
shows every reading beyond the leg + 60 mm or OUT_OF_RANGE; the servo is centred and the front monitor active 
during all forward travel (PASS moves in 50 mm steps, re-centring after each side look). Reverse ≤ 120 mm at ≤ 100 
mm/s, only along the path just driven.
FR9 Publish MQTT telemetry at 5 Hz, events when they occur, and a 1 Hz heartbeat with link statistics.
FR10
Accept start, stop, calibrate and tuning commands (speed=, pid=, ff=, line=, rate=) over MQTT, USB or UART. Reply to 
each with its request id: accepted or rejected (with reason), then applied once the owning task confirms. When queues 
are full, reject everything except STOP.
FR11 Recover the network without blocking driving: reconnect to the broker after a TCP / MQTT failure (implemented) and 
re-join the access point after 3 s of link loss, retrying after 5 s, 10 s, then every 20 s (designed, not implemented).
FR12
START (GP20) and STOP (GP21). STOP from the button, USB, UART or MQTT latches, brakes and cancels the 
current move, scan, calibration and pending commands in every state, and never starts motion. Only an accepted 
START clears the latch: a short press (acted on release) or "start" begins a run; a ≥ 2 s hold selects calibration. START 
is accepted only if its health check passes (section 5); otherwise the car stays braked and latched.
FR13
Calibrate with a slow 360° spin (from IDLE by "calibrate" or a long START; from STOPPED by a long START), 
recording IR white / black levels and magnetometer offsets. Keep the new IR levels only if every sensor's contrast is ≥ 
50 % of the ADC range. STOP aborts it.
FR14
Fail safe: brake when a velocity request is older than 100 ms; the motion task feeds a 250 ms RP2040 watchdog only 
while the vehicle task has made progress within 200 ms; after any reset the car starts braked and never starts a run by 
itself.
 
1.2 Non-Functional Requirements
ID Requirement
NFR1 Wheel-speed control every 20 ms (50 Hz), woken by a kernel cyclic handler; jitter < 2 ms with Wi-Fi telemetry running 
(A2).
NFR2
IR sampled at 1 kHz by a timer ISR, giving ≥ 10 samples on a 3 mm bar up to 300 mm/s (about 17 at the default 180 
mm/s). The limit applies to the barcode sensor, including turns: with sensor offsets x_bc (forward) and y_bc (left), the 
vehicle task keeps √((v − ω·y_bc)² + (ω·x_bc)²) ≤ 300 mm/s, and speed= above 300 mm/s is rejected (the code 
currently accepts 400 mm/s).
NFR3
STOP brakes the motors within 100 ms of the button edge or of a complete command arriving, in every state, under 
full load and with command queues full. The GP21 edge IRQ, the USB and MQTT receive paths (before their 
command rings) and the telemetry task's UART poll set the same latch; motion checks it every 20 ms. Target until 
measured (A14); an MQTT STOP needs a live connection.
NFR4 Confirm line loss over 150 ms and junctions over 10 mm of travel, so one noisy sample or tape gap cannot trigger an 
action.
NFR5
Front sonar ranges a ≥ 50 mm-wide box from 20 to 400 mm within ±10 mm; a front reading every 70 ms while driving 
forward with the servo centred (≥ 60 ms between pings, 40 ms echo timeout). "Clear" needs two front readings ≥ 360 
mm or OUT_OF_RANGE (A9); a front reading older than 200 ms while driving forward is a sensor fault. Stop budget 
at 300 mm/s: two readings + vehicle + motion cycles = 180 ms ≈ 54 mm, + braking ≤ 40 mm (A3) + 10 mm error, so 
the car stops about 190 mm from the box.
NFR6
Read fresh, coherent IMU samples every 20 ms on I2C0 at 400 kHz (10 ms I2C timeout; three stale or failed reads = 
sensor fault). Hump height within ±5 mm of a ruler on every measured course pass — not yet demonstrated (A7, 
A12).
NFR7 Straight moves within ±2 % (±10 mm over 500 mm); 90° turns within ±5° on the course surface.
NFR8 Only the cyw43 task calls lwIP. Connection windows 2 / 4 / 8 s; PINGREQ after 30 s idle; session dropped after 45 s 
with nothing received. A network stall never blocks driving.
NFR9
Every wait is bounded and ends on STOP: encoder 200 ms, echo 40 ms, sonar semaphore 100 ms, move 3 × ideal 
time + 1 s (≤ 10 s), scan 12 s; vehicle-task waits are sliced ≤ 50 ms. motion_wait() returns OK, FAIL or CANCELLED; 
only OK may start the next move.
NFR10
C99 and BARR-C:2018: zero warnings under -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Wsign-conversion -Wshadow and 0 
tools/barr_check.py findings, deviations documented. Unverified until the reviewed commit, build configuration, 
compiler log and checker output are attached.
NFR11
Static allocation only (no application malloc). Last linked size ≈ 361 KB flash and 58 KB RAM (.data + .bss, not run-
time use); attach the size output for the reviewed commit and keep ≥ 25 % free in every task stack (measured high-
water mark).

Priority and ownership justification:
Priorities follow deadlines and consequences (lower number = higher priority in µT-Kernel):
· ISRs and the cyclic handler only record data, set the stop latch or wake a task; they never block.
· motion (5) has the hardest deadline: a late 20 ms cycle makes the wheels surge, it is the only motor owner, it 
brakes on the stop latch and it feeds the watchdog.
· vehicle (6) makes the 20 ms driving decisions and issues motion requests, so it sits directly below motion.
· barcode (7) runs every 10 ms below vehicle: edge timing is done in the ISR and the 64-entry ring holds two full 
barcodes, so the task can run late without losing data.
· imu (8) is soft real-time: hump estimation works on encoder distance, and stale samples are detected by the 
status bits.
· obstacle (9) is limited by the sensor (≥ 60 ms between pings); the 70 ms period meets the NFR5 stop budget.
· telemetry (10) is human-facing; its queues drop and count rather than block. STOP does not depend on it except 
for UART.
· usb (3) and cyw43 (4) keep the template priorities their drivers need. They pre-empt motion, so their effect on 
jitter and STOP latency is measured (A2, A14) and cyw43 moves below motion if needed.
Ownership: one owner per peripheral (motors and watchdog: motion; IR ADC: ir_sample_isr; I2C0: imu; network: 
cyw43). The sonar and servo are shared by the obstacle monitor and vehicle scans under one semaphore held for 
each aim + settle + ping.

Brief explanation of the main communication paths:
· Sensor → ISR → task. ir_sample_isr writes the IR snapshot and pushes bar / space widths into a 64-entry 
single-producer / single-consumer ring for the barcode task; encoder_edge_isr updates counts for motion; 
echo_edge_isr sets the echo flag (40 ms timeout); stop_btn_isr sets the stop latch.
· Control loop. The 20 ms cyclic handler wakes motion (tk_wup_tsk). The vehicle task posts time-stamped move / 
velocity / stop requests (newest wins; velocity expires after 100 ms) and waits on the done flag, which returns OK, 
FAIL or CANCELLED.
· STOP path. GP21 IRQ, the usb and cyw43 receive paths (before their rings) and the telemetry UART poll set one 
latch, independent of any queue. Motion brakes on its next cycle, completes the move as CANCELLED and 
refuses new ones; the vehicle task (waits sliced ≤ 50 ms) goes to STOPPED and clears pending commands. 
Only an accepted START clears the latch.
· Barcode → navigation. The barcode task sends each decoded command once through a 4-message buffer; the 
vehicle task polls it without blocking.
· Snapshots (dashed). Status structs are copied with interrupts briefly disabled and single words read directly; 
reads never block (single-core profile, A13). The only blocking waits are the bounded ones in NFR9.
· Commands and replies. The telemetry task parses commands and routes them to their owner through a non-
blocking message buffer (full → "rejected: busy"). Replies carry the request id and use a 4-slot priority queue sent 
before telemetry, retried every 500 ms up to 3 times; a repeated id (last 16, kept 30 s after completion) returns the 
recorded result without re-running. Telemetry uses a 6-slot ring; drops are counted in the heartbeat.
· Sonar and watchdog. The sonar semaphore covers each aim + settle + ping and enforces ≥ 60 ms between 
pings. Motion feeds the 250 ms watchdog only while the vehicle task has made progress within 200 ms.
 
5. System-Level Statechart
Model the operating states of the entire system. Label transition events or conditions. Include relevant fault, stop, or 
recovery behaviour.
 
Explain the main states and any important assumptions:
The vehicle task (vehicle.c, 20 ms) owns these states and checks the buttons, the stop latch and commands in every 
state.
· IDLE — power-on state: braked, servo centred, unlatched. Short START or "start" → LINE_FOLLOW [run check], 
resetting the run statistics; long START or "calibrate" → CALIBRATE [calibration check].
· CALIBRATE — slow 360° spin recording IR min / max and magnetometer offsets; new IR levels kept only if 
every sensor's contrast is ≥ 50 %; then IDLE.
· RUNNING (composite):
· LINE_FOLLOW — PID steering; watches barcodes, the front range and IMU impacts. A pending L / R / straight 
command expires after 400 mm without a junction.
· NAV_TURN — at a junction with a command pending (U-turn at once). L / R: advance 70 mm, turn 90°, re-
centre; U-turn: turn 180°, re-centre; straight: drive 90 mm across.
· LINE_SEARCH — both sensors white for 150 ms, or a successful move that ends off the line: sweep k = 1 … 5 
to ±k·30° of the lost heading, alternating, first towards the last-seen side; IR ≥ 0.15 → re-centre → 
LINE_FOLLOW; five sweeps without the line → STOPPED "line not found".
· OBSTACLE — stop, scan, plan, then bypass, reverse and re-plan, or give up (6.6).
· STOPPED — braked and latched; pending commands cleared; reason published (stop command, path blocked, 
line not found, motion fault, sensor fault). Short START [run check] → new run; long START [calibration check] → 
CALIBRATE; the "calibrate" command is rejected while latched.
Global: STOP in any state (IDLE and CALIBRATE included), a failed move (FAIL) or a sensor fault → STOPPED; 
faults never start a line search. [run check]: valid IR calibration, fresh IMU, front reading ≥ 360 mm or 
OUT_OF_RANGE, no unresolved motion fault. [calibration check]: fresh IMU, healthy motion, clear front and spin 
footprint (A10). A failed check rejects START with its reason and leaves the car braked and latched; "start" while 
running is rejected. Assumptions: the car starts on the line; a junction is both sensors black for ≥ 10 mm.

Field Student Entry
Subsystem name MQTT connection manager (mqtt_lwip.c) on lwIP's MQTT client (lwip/src/apps/mqtt)
Related RTOS task(s) cyw43 template task (priority 4) through the cyw43_utk_app_poll() hook; telemetry task 
(priority 10) through the TX / command rings
Purpose
Keep one MQTT 3.1.1 session to the laptop broker, publish replies and telemetry, deliver 
commands, and recover from broker / TCP failures (implemented) and access-point loss 
(WIFI_REJOIN, designed) without blocking driving (FR9–FR11, NFR8).
Reason a statechart is needed
The client is event-driven (lwIP callbacks) and has timeouts and retries; a statechart makes 
each failure path and its recovery explicit so no state can hang and the dashboard can show 
the link state.
 
Brief explanation of states, events, and fault handling:
· WAIT_NET — no link or no DHCP address; nothing sent. Link + IP → CONNECTING; link down ≥ 3 s → 
WIFI_REJOIN.
· WIFI_REJOIN (designed, not implemented) — non-blocking cyw43_wifi_join() from the poll hook; retry after 5 s, 
10 s, then every 20 s; re-joins counted in the heartbeat.
· CONNECTING — TCP + CONNECT with retained last will "offline"; attempt windows 2 / 4 / 8 s. CONNACK → 
CONNECTED: publish "online" (retained), subscribe picocar/<id>/cmd, window back to 2 s.
· CONNECTED — each 2 ms poll sends replies first, then telemetry. Incoming STOP sets the latch; other 
commands go to the command ring. PINGREQ after 30 s idle; session dropped after 45 s with nothing received.
· BACKOFF — refused CONNACK, TCP error, peer close or keep-alive timeout: wait for the current window, then 
retry. Link or IP lost → WAIT_NET.
State and recovery counts appear in the heartbeat; messages published while disconnected are dropped and 
counted, and replies that never got through are reported as unconfirmed. FR11 stays incomplete until broker loss 
(stop mosquitto 60 s) and AP loss (power-cycle the hotspot) each pass separately.
6.2 Subsystem Statechart 2 — Motion Controller (Buddy 2)
Field Student Entry
Subsystem name Motion controller (motion.c with pid.c, motor.c, encoder.c)
Related RTOS task(s) motion task (priority 5), woken every 20 ms by a kernel cyclic handler; requests come from 
the vehicle task (and the test programs)
Purpose
Turn distance, angle and speed requests into wheel PWM (per-wheel PID with feed-forward), 
end every move as OK, FAIL or CANCELLED, brake on the stop latch or a stale request, and 
feed the watchdog (FR4, FR14, NFR1, NFR7, NFR9).
Reason a statechart is needed The controller behaves differently by mode (idle, velocity, open loop, moving with ramp-
down) and must end every move in a defined way (done, stopped or failed); a statechart 
shows each mode, the completion rules and the stall timeout.
 
Brief explanation of states, events, and fault handling:
· IDLE — targets 0, PID reset, braked; new moves refused while latched. After any reset the task stays here, 
braked, and arms only after the first vehicle check-in.
· VELOCITY — time-stamped wheel speeds from the vehicle task; older than 100 ms → brake, FAIL "stale 
command". OPEN_LOOP — fixed duty, TEST_MOTOR only.
· MOVING — CRUISE at the requested speed with straightness correction; RAMP_DOWN when < 80 mm (or 
equivalent arc) remains, towards 60 mm/s.
· Results — remaining ≤ 4 mm: OK. Timeout (3 × ideal + 1 s) or non-finite goal: brake, FAIL → vehicle goes to 
STOPPED "motion fault". motion_stop() or the stop latch: brake, CANCELLED → the vehicle starts nothing.
Every cycle: check the latch, read the encoders (both edges of the 20-slot disc, 40 counts / rev ≈ 5 mm per count; 
speed from edge times, 0 after 200 ms without an edge), update odometry and heading, run the PIDs, publish status, 
feed the watchdog only if vehicle progress is < 200 ms old. Single-channel encoders: direction follows the 
commanded sign; before reversing, the old sign is kept while braked until 250 ms pass with no edge and residual 
speed ≈ 0 (FAIL if not settled within 1 s).
6.3 Subsystem Statechart 3 — Line Tracking Classifier (Buddy 3)
Field Student Entry
Subsystem name Line tracking classifier (line_follow.c)
Related RTOS task(s) vehicle task (priority 6), every 20 ms; IR data from ir_sample_isr (1 kHz)
Purpose Classify the two normalised IR readings as on the line, line lost or junction, and compute the 
PID steering and speed while on the line (FR1, FR2, NFR4).
Reason a statechart is needed Line loss and junctions must be confirmed over time or distance, not from one sample; the 
statechart makes these confirmation rules and the recovery back to LINE_ON explicit.
 
Brief explanation of states, events, and fault handling:
· LINE_ON — normal tracking: error e = L − R (0 = white, 1 = black), steering = PID(e) limited to ±250 mm/s, 
forward speed = cruise × (1 − 0.5 |e|) so the car slows in bends.
· LINE_LOST — both readings below 0.15 for at least 150 ms. The classifier gives no steering here: the vehicle 
task stops the car and searches (LINE_SEARCH), sweeping first towards the side the line was last seen. Any 
reading of 0.15 or more returns to LINE_ON.
· LINE_JUNCTION — both readings above 0.75 for at least 10 mm of travel (a cross line or T). Either reading 
dropping to 0.75 or below returns to LINE_ON.
The vehicle task reacts to the classifier: LINE_LOST → system state LINE_SEARCH; LINE_JUNCTION with a 
pending barcode command → NAV_TURN. The time and distance thresholds stop a noisy sample or a short gap in 
the tape from causing an action. Calibration (CALIBRATE state) sets the white / black levels used for normalising.
6.4 Subsystem Statechart 4 — Barcode Capture and Decoding (Buddy 3)
Field Student Entry
Subsystem name Barcode capture and decoding (ir_sensor.c edge detector, barcode.c, barcode_decode.c)
Related RTOS task(s) ir_sample_isr (1 kHz, region A); barcode task (priority 7, every 10 ms, region B); result to the 
vehicle task through a message buffer
Purpose Measure bar and space widths from the barcode IR sensor, assemble a frame, decode *X* in 
either reading direction and send the navigation command (FR3, NFR2).
Reason a statechart is needed Capture (ISR) and decoding (task) run concurrently; the statechart shows both regions, the 
frame rules (bar-aligned 29-element window, 250 ms reset) and every rejection path.
 
Brief explanation of states, events, and fault handling:
· Region A (ISR) — WHITE / BLACK with 0.6 / 0.4 hysteresis; each change pushes the element and its width (µs) 
into the 64-entry lock-free ring. Ring overflow invalidates the current frame.
· Region B (task) — QUIET until the first bar, then COLLECTING (≤ 79 elements). From the 29th element, each 
new element triggers DECODING of the last 29, starting and ending on a bar; an invalid window slides by one bar 
+ space. White ≥ 250 ms, a runaway or an overflow discards the frame.
· DECODING — the 3 widest of each 9 elements are wide; Code 39 lookup forwards, then reversed; must be *X* 
with wide / narrow ratio ≥ 1.6. A–D are sent once per frame (frame id); other valid symbols are reported only. Bad 
ratio, unknown symbol or missing * are counted.
· Timing — decoding without the quiet zone gives a nominal 10 ms (task) + 20 ms (vehicle poll) ≈ 30 ms ≈ 9 mm at 
300 mm/s, against 250 ms ≈ 75 mm before. The loaded worst case is still to be measured (A11).
6.5 Subsystem Statechart 5 — Hump Tracker (Buddy 4)
Field Student Entry
Subsystem name Hump tracker (terrain.c)
Related RTOS task(s) imu task (priority 8), every 20 ms; distance from the motion status (encoders); hump events 
published by the telemetry task
Purpose Estimate the leading-contact height from pitch and encoder distance, detect and count 
humps and report the peak (FR5, NFR6).
Reason a statechart is needed
Detection, peak and end are decided from noisy pitch over distance, and the height history 
must be valid; the statechart fixes the trigger, end, drift reset, abandon and history-invalid 
rules.
 
Brief explanation of states, events, and fault handling:
· Estimator — body pitch θ is the slope between the trailing and leading contacts, a wheelbase L apart (130 mm 
nominal, to be measured), so h_f(s) = h_f(s − L) + L · sin θa(s), where s is encoder distance and θa is pitch 
averaged over the last 25 mm. h_f(s − L) comes from a 4 mm-bin distance ring with interpolation; the car's own 
acceleration is removed before computing pitch. It replaces the ds · sin(pitch) integral, which read a 40 × 200 mm 
hump as about 29 mm.
· NO_HISTORY → FLAT — counting starts only after L of valid forward travel. A turn, reverse or IMU fault discards 
the history and any candidate.
· FLAT — h_f tracked; reset to 0 after L of travel with |θ| < 1.5°. ON_HUMP — h_f ≥ 10 mm (height trigger above 
the 7 mm simulated flat-floor noise; detects humps ≥ 15 mm with margin, A12; the old 6° pitch trigger missed a 20 
× 600 mm hump peaking at 5.98°); peak = max h_f. End — h_f < 3 mm for L: count it and publish. Abandon — > 
1.5 m on one hump.
· IMU set-up — accelerometer 100 Hz, ±2 g, high-resolution, block data update; magnetometer 75 Hz, ±1.3 gauss. 
ZYXDA / DRDY checked each read; three stale or failed reads → sensor fault → STOPPED. Turn rate from the 
encoder differential, cross-checked with the wrapped (±180°) magnetometer heading change, which is ignored 
when |B| leaves ±25 % of calibration (A16).
· Evidence — host simulation only (rigid two-contact model, 100 runs per case, 100–300 mm/s, humps 10–40 mm 
× 200–600 mm): 95th-percentile error ≤ 1.6 mm at 0.5° pitch noise and ≤ 4.6 mm at 1°; flat floor at 1° gave false 
peaks up to 7 mm, hence the 10 mm trigger. The script is committed with the evidence; NFR6 is met only after 
the real-hump test (A7).
6.6 Subsystem Statechart 6 — Obstacle Handling (Buddy 5, with Buddy 2)
Field Student Entry
Subsystem name Obstacle handling (vehicle.c handle_obstacle, obstacle.c, avoidance.c, ultrasonic.c, servo.c)
Related RTOS task(s) vehicle task (priority 6) runs the sequence; obstacle task (priority 9) monitors the front range; 
motion task (priority 5) executes the moves
Purpose Stop before an obstacle, profile it, choose bypass / reverse / stop, pass it with the smaller 
offset and rejoin the line, never driving on unchecked or faulty sensing (FR7, FR8, NFR5).
Reason a statechart is needed
A multi-step sequence with guards, a shared retry budget and several ways to fail; the 
statechart shows each decision and where each failure leads (STOPPED, or LINE_SEARCH 
only after a successful rejoin that misses the line).
 
Brief explanation of states, events, and fault handling:
· Ping classes — VALID (20–4000 mm), OUT_OF_RANGE (echo pulse longer than 4 m: counted as free space 
only if A9 confirms the module gives this pulse when nothing is in range), FAULT (no echo pulse). Two FAULTs 
in a row, or a front reading older than 200 ms while driving forward → STOPPED "sensor fault".
· STOP_SETTLE → SCAN → PLAN — brake, wait 200 ms (after an IMU impact, reverse 120 mm first: a touching 
box is inside the 20 mm blind zone). SCAN runs braked with the front monitor paused: coarse 30–150°, fine ±20° 
in 5° steps, 3 valid pings per angle in ≤ 5 tries, whole scan ≤ 12 s. avoidance.c turns the points into edges and 
side clearances.
· [clear] — every corridor angle (±140 mm = half the car width + 60 mm) is VALID beyond the corridor or 
OUT_OF_RANGE, and two front readings are ≥ 360 mm or OUT_OF_RANGE → LINE_FOLLOW.
· [back off] — closest < 90 mm or no side with ≥ 220 mm: REVERSE 120 mm at ≤ 100 mm/s along the path just 
driven, then re-scan; reverses and re-plans share 2 attempts, then [blocked] → STOPPED "path blocked".
· [side free] → BYPASS — SIDESTEP (turn 90°, leg check, drive the offset, turn back), PASS (leg check; 50 mm 
steps, each followed by a braked side look and re-centring, until the obstacle is seen and gone, ≤ 600 mm, then 
one car length), RETURN (turn, leg check, drive until an IR sensor sees the line, ≤ offset + 200 mm), REJOIN 
(leg check, forward 70 mm, turn 90°, sweep until centred) → LINE_FOLLOW. Leg check: 70°, 90°, 110° all 
beyond leg + 60 mm or OUT_OF_RANGE, else re-plan (uses an attempt). The servo is centred and the front 
monitor active during all forward travel.
· Faults — STOP, a failed move or a sensor fault → STOPPED. Only a successful rejoin that ends off the line 
leads to LINE_SEARCH. Corners closer than about 150 mm outside the leg-check cone, and the swept area of 
turns, are not covered yet; validation planned (A10).

9. Design Consistency
Use this section to demonstrate that the design artefacts describe one coherent system rather than independent 
diagrams.

Which task owns each hardware device?
Motors (GP8–11) and watchdog: motion. Encoders (GP2 / GP3): 
encoder_edge_isr → motion. IR (GP26–28): ir_sample_isr → vehicle and 
barcode. IMU (I2C0, GP4 / GP5): imu. HC-SR04 (GP6 / GP7) and servo 
(GP12): obstacle monitor and vehicle scans under one semaphore; 
echo_edge_isr times the echo. START (GP20): vehicle. STOP (GP21): 
stop_btn_isr → stop latch → motion. Wi-Fi: cyw43. USB / UART0: usb 
and telemetry. LED (GP16): blink.
Which task has the highest priority, and why?
Motion (5) is the highest application task: it owns the motors, runs the 20 
ms loop, brakes on the latch and feeds the watchdog. ISRs and the cyclic 
handler run above it but only record data or signal. The template usb (3) 
and cyw43 (4) also pre-empt it; their effect is measured (A2, A14).
Which subsystem statechart corresponds to which 
RTOS task or subsystem?
6.1 MQTT: cyw43 with telemetry. 6.2 Motion: motion. 6.3 Line classifier: 
vehicle (line_follow.c). 6.4 Barcode: ir_sample_isr and barcode. 6.5 Hump 
tracker: imu (terrain.c). 6.6 Obstacle: vehicle with obstacle and motion. 
Section 5 is the vehicle task's state machine; OBSTACLE is expanded in 
6.6, and LINE_SEARCH is defined in section 5 using the 6.3 classifier.
How are functional requirements reflected in the task 
and statechart design?
FR1–2: vehicle, LINE_FOLLOW / LINE_SEARCH, 6.3. FR3: 
ir_sample_isr + barcode (6.4), NAV_TURN. FR4: motion (6.2). FR5–6: 
imu (6.5). FR7–8: OBSTACLE (6.6) with obstacle and motion. FR9–11: 
telemetry and cyw43 (6.1). FR12–13: stop latch, START checks, 
STOPPED and CALIBRATE (section 5). FR14: motion watchdog and 
vehicle progress.
