CSC05

This is feedback by lecturer after week 6 design review.

This is an exceptionally thorough and coherent design. The requirements are quantified, ISRs only record data, every bounded wait is listed (NFR9), there is a single motor owner, and the STOP latch is independent of the queues. The risk has shifted from design quality to validation and integration, because much of the design is still "designed, not implemented" or tested in host simulation only.
- The pin selection is sound. It handles the Grove pitfalls explicitly: IR jumpered off Grove 5/6 to avoid the GP26 duplicate, the VBAT jumper kept open for GP28, I2C0 on GP4/5, and the servo on GP12 away from the motor slices. Since the encoder, echo and STOP ISRs all share the IO_BANK0 vector, keep them in one short dispatcher.
- A9 is the most important assumption. The HC-SR04 runs at 3.3 V against a 5 V datasheet, and the VALID / OUT_OF_RANGE / FAULT classification and FR7 depend on its no-target behaviour. Test this first, and decide on the 5 V plus divider fallback before writing obstacle code.
- The ISR/task split is correct. However, the template usb (3) and cyw43 (4) tasks pre-empt motion (5), and the M0+ has no FPU. Measure WCET for motion, vehicle, barcode and the hump estimator, plus the cost of the 1 kHz ir_sample_isr, and report total CPU load alongside the A2 jitter test.
- Counting both edges of the 20-slot disc doubles resolution, but slot duty is rarely 50%, so speed from successive edges will alternate. Use same-edge periods or average over a full slot, and check that the 300 µs glitch filter is below the shortest half-period at maximum speed.
- The ±1.6–4.6 mm hump error comes from a rigid two-contact simulation. Confirm which contact leads in each direction, measure L, and check that wheel slip does not corrupt the distance axis. Decide what you will report if NFR6 (±5 mm) is not met.
- The obstacle sequence is safe but slow: scans take up to 12 s, followed by 50 mm PASS steps and up to two re-plans. Estimate the worst-case time against the course limit and the watchdog/progress rules. Corners outside the leg-check cone are not yet covered (A10).
- The barcode sensor's side flips after a U-turn (A11). Confirm the course layout now, because a second sensor would change the pin map, ISR and timing budget.
- Restrict FR10 tuning commands (pid=, speed= etc.) to IDLE/STOPPED. Publish the full topic list and JSON schemas, not just the command topic.
- Consolidate the fault paths, which are spread over FR7, FR14, NFR6, NFR9 and Sections 5–6, into one table (detection, latency, response, recovery, reason string) that can serve as the A14 checklist.
- WIFI_REJOIN is not yet implemented. Because cyw43 sits above motion, make sure the rejoin in the poll hook is genuinely non-blocking, and measure its effect during AP loss.
- Scope is the main risk, with 16 open assumptions and pending code. Validate A9, A2/A14, A3, A6 and A7 first, and keep a working integrated baseline at each step.
Key Risks to Validate Early
1. HC-SR04 behaviour at 3.3 V, including the no-target pulse (A9).
2. Control-loop jitter, CPU load and STOP latency with usb/cyw43 pre-empting motion.
3. Real-hump accuracy of the two-contact estimator (±5 mm).
4. Barcode decoding at up to 300 mm/s, in both directions and after U-turns.
5. Total obstacle bypass time and clearance (A10).

The design is in excellent shape. Put your effort into early hardware validation of its assumptions rather than more design detail.
